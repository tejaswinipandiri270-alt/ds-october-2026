2 · Prototype vs PoC vs MVP

People often mix these three up. Each answers a different question, and only the MVP goes to real users.

PrototypeProof of concept (PoC)MVP
Question it answersWhat could this look like? What does the data tell us?Can the risky core idea actually work?Will real users get value from it?
Who sees itThe teamThe team and stakeholdersReal users
Code qualityThrowaway (notebooks, mock-ups)Rough, but on real dataProduction quality: tested, versioned, monitored
OutcomeIdeas and directionA go / no-go decisionThe first release, v1.0
Data science exampleEDA notebook and a quick baseline model for churnThe model beats the current rule-based approach on last year's dataWeekly churn-risk list sent to the retention team
MVP = Minimum Viable Product: the smallest version that solves a real problem for real users. It's small on features, not on quality: an MVP is still tested and monitored.

4 · Waterfall vs Agile

Both go through the same stages. Waterfall does each stage once, in order, for the whole product. Agile runs short cycles (sprints) that each go through every stage for a small slice of the product. Switch between them, then press Requirements change! to see what happens when the client changes their mind in month 7.

Value in users' hands over time
WaterfallAgile
PlanEverything up frontA little at a time; the backlog evolves
First releaseAt the endEarly (MVP), then frequent updates
ChangeExpensive; may mean going back stagesExpected; goes into the next sprint
TestingA phase near the endEvery sprint, ideally automated
Customer involvementAt the start and the endThroughout, at every sprint review
DocumentationHeavy, formal sign-offsLighter, just enough

When to use which

Waterfall suits: fixed, well-understood requirements; heavy regulation or contracts; hardware or construction, where changing late is physically costly.

Agile suits: uncertain requirements; products that need user feedback; and data science and AI work, where you can't know how good a model will be until you try.

In practice many organisations mix the two: a fixed overall plan and budget (Waterfall-ish) delivered in sprints (Agile).
